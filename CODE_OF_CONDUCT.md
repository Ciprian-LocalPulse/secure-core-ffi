# Code of Conduct

## Purpose

`security-core-ffi` is a small, independently maintained project, but the standard it holds itself to — technical rigor, honest documentation of limitations, and openness to scrutiny — extends to how people are expected to treat each other around it. This document sets out what that looks like in practice, for issues, pull requests, discussions, and any other space connected to this project.

## Our standard

Participation in this project should be:

- **Substantive.** Disagreement about design, architecture, or code is not just tolerated here, it is invited — see the [Manifesto](MANIFESTO.md). The expectation is that disagreement engages with the actual technical claim being made, not with the person making it.
- **Precise.** Bug reports, security concerns, and design critiques are most useful when they are specific and reproducible. Vague criticism is harder to act on than a pointed one, even a harsh one.
- **Respectful of time and effort.** This project is maintained by one person without institutional backing. That doesn't exempt the work from criticism, but it does mean that contributors and reporters are expected to engage in good faith rather than treat maintenance as an obligation owed to them.

The following are not acceptable in any space connected to this project:

- Personal attacks, insults, or derogatory comments directed at any contributor, maintainer, or user — including based on the quality of a question asked or a mistake made.
- Harassment of any kind, public or private, including unwelcome contact after being asked to stop.
- Discriminatory language or behavior related to age, body size, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, level of experience, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity or orientation.
- Publishing others' private information — including physical or email addresses — without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately submitting misleading vulnerability reports, false claims about the project's security properties, or bad-faith bug reports intended to waste maintainer time rather than improve the project.
- Any other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional technical setting.

## Scope

This code of conduct applies within all project spaces — the repository's issues, pull requests, discussions, and wiki/documentation pages — and also applies when an individual is representing the project in public spaces, such as using an official project email address or speaking on behalf of the project at an event.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness behavior that violates this code of conduct, report it by contacting the maintainer directly at **contact@agentflow-enterprise.com**. Reports will be handled with discretion; the maintainer will not disclose the identity of a reporter without their consent, except where required by law.

**This contact address is for conduct concerns only.** To report a security vulnerability in the code itself, use the process described in `SECURITY.md` (GitHub Security Advisories), not this address.

## Enforcement

Instances of unacceptable behavior may result in a warning, temporary or permanent removal of comments, contributions, or issues, and — for repeated or severe violations — a ban from further interaction with the project, at the maintainer's discretion. Given the project's size, enforcement decisions are made by the maintainer directly rather than by a separate committee; this document is the standard those decisions will be measured against.

## A note on independent, single-maintainer projects

This project does not have the staffing of a large open-source foundation, and enforcement here will be handled personally rather than by a dedicated moderation team. That is disclosed here plainly, in the same spirit as the rest of this project's documentation: stating what the project is and is not, rather than implying a level of infrastructure that doesn't exist.
